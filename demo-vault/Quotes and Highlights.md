# Quotes and Highlights

How Glacial Bloom handles blockquotes, nested quotes, and text highlights.

## Blockquotes

> This is a blockquote. The left border uses the glacial border color, and the text is slightly muted compared to body text. Blockquotes are useful for pulling out a passage from another source.

> This is a longer blockquote that wraps across multiple lines. Notice how the left border stays consistent and the text maintains comfortable line height. The muted text color helps the quote feel distinct from the body without disappearing entirely.

## Nested quotes

## Blockquote with formatting

> **Bold quote.** You can use *italic*, `inline code`, [links](Welcome), and ==highlights== inside blockquotes.
>
> Multiple paragraphs work too — just use a blank line and a `>` prefix.

## Highlights

The signature warm red bloom is Glacial Bloom's defining feature. Highlights use muted red (`#dc2626` at 35% opacity) instead of the default yellow.

==This is a highlighted passage.== Notice how the red tint is warm but not aggressive — it draws the eye without shouting.

Here's a paragraph with ==a highlighted phrase== in the middle, followed by more text. The highlight integrates with the flow rather than breaking it.

==You can highlight entire sentences or even multiple sentences. The red tint stays consistent across line breaks and paragraph boundaries.==

## Blockquote + highlight combination

> ==A highlighted passage inside a blockquote.== The combination is rare but works — the red tint sits on top of the muted quote background, creating a layered effect.

## Inline highlights in context

When you read a long passage, you might want to ==mark key phrases for later review==. The red tint is distinctive enough to find with a quick scan, but subtle enough not to break your reading flow. This is ==particularly useful for research notes== where you're pulling out citations from multiple sources.
